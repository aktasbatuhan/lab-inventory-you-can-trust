The point isn't a single number. It's that the design was tested against the kind of mess a real lab makes, at the scale Adaptyv runs. The simulation also tested the schema's speed: at four weeks of data, the "what's usable" question took 77 seconds. Profiling found two lookups that ran once per row, and the fix made it 1.5 seconds with identical answers. Across three random seeds at 8 weeks and 2,000 designs a week, normal noise produced 117 to 162 mistakes with 6 to 11 left silently wrong; bad-week noise produced about 600 with around 50 silently wrong. Usable target always ended slightly under the truth, between 9 and 81 µL, because uncertain tubes are held back rather than counted. Expressed protein was overstated by 14 to 33 percent, all evaporation. What stays silently wrong is honest: if nobody scans a bin, no system can know. The answer there is to make the common case automatic, as we did for single-use plates, and to surface things nobody has seen in a while.
