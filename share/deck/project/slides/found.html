What the tests and agents caught

What was wrongWhat changed
Rules were stamped with the moment the file loaded, so every question about September saw no shelf-life rulesRules carry the date they started
“A month in the fridge” was ambiguous once a tube went back to the freezerStorage rules became time budgets
A lost robot log line left the source well looking fuller than it wasFinished runs assume their plan, aborted runs don’t
Counting every tube in a box didn’t confirm the box; a typed-in move looked as solid as a scanned oneSeeing a tube confirms its box; typed moves read “placed, not confirmed”

Agents weren’t allowed to edit the schema. They wrote up what they found, and each fix was a decision made on purpose. Eleven decisions are written down with their reasons.

I think this slide says more about the method than any other. Tests caught two of these, and reading real answers from the demo lab caught the other two. None of them would have shown up by looking at the schema. Every finding and every fix is in the journal folder of the repo, with the agent's original write-up next to it.
