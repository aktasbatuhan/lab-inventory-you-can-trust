The inventory is what everything else trusts

The brief says it plainly: "Recording the facts correctly matters more than any single feature." I read the case as a data-integrity problem first and an inventory problem second. When the inventory is wrong, three things happen, straight from the brief: material we own gets reordered because the records say it's used up, plates already in the bin still look usable because nobody recorded throwing them away, and a bad batch of target can't be traced to the experiments it touched. Each one is the records drifting away from what really happened, with nobody able to tell. So the question I designed around was: how do you keep the records honest when many hands, robots and scanners write to them, and people make mistakes?
